# Explanation

## Approach

Use each possible center as the origin of an expansion. An odd-length palindrome is centered on one character, so begin with `left = right = center`. An even-length palindrome is centered between two characters, so begin with `left = center` and `right = center + 1`.

At every step, if the two boundary characters match, the current section is a palindrome; count it and widen the section by one character on each side. The first mismatch, or an out-of-range boundary, stops that expansion.

## Correctness

Every non-empty palindromic slice has exactly one center: its middle character when its length is odd, or the gap between its two middle characters when its length is even. The corresponding expansion starts at that center and grows outward. The slice is counted precisely after all of its outer pairs have matched, which happens exactly when the slice is a palindrome. Expansions stop at the first mismatch or boundary, so no non-palindrome is counted. Since each slice has one unique center, it is generated by exactly one expansion and counted once.

## Complexity

- Time: `O(n^2)` in the worst case, such as a string of identical characters where every expansion reaches a boundary.
- Space: `O(1)`, because the expansions use only two indices.

## Common mistakes

- Expanding only around single characters and missing even-length palindromes.
- Counting a center twice by using overlapping even-center conventions.
- Continuing an expansion after a mismatch.
- Creating every substring eagerly, which costs extra time and memory.

## Related pattern

Center expansion also solves longest-palindromic-substring and palindrome-counting variants.
